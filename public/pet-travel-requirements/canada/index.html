Canada Pet Travel Requirements From the United States

For personal travel with a dog or cat from the United States to Canada, the key questions are the pet's age, current rabies documentation, whether the animal is traveling with its owner, and whether the trip is personal or commercial.

Reviewed by YourPetPass Travel Desk · Last reviewed September 28, 2026

Requirements at a glance

  • Scope: this page covers personal dogs and cats traveling from the United States to Canada.
  • Rabies: dogs and cats 3 months of age or older generally need acceptable current rabies documentation when arriving from the United States, which Canada does not list as rabies-free.
  • Young pets: dogs and cats under 3 months are not subject to the standard rabies-vaccination requirement in CFIA's reference rules.
  • Language: the rabies documentation used under the standard pathway should be in English or French and clearly identify the animal.
  • No routine post-import quarantine for pet dogs: CFIA's current reference document states that pet dogs imported from any country are not subject to post-import quarantine.
  • Transit counts: Canada's import requirements also apply to animals transiting Canada on the way to another destination.

Direct answer: for most personal U.S.-to-Canada trips with a dog or cat 3 months of age or older, the simplest path is to travel with a valid rabies vaccination certificate that clearly identifies the animal and shows the pet is currently vaccinated. Age, ownership/travel arrangement, commercial purpose and recent origin can change the rule, so this page should not be used for resale/adoption or other commercial imports.

For U.S. → Canada pet travel, the first useful check is simple: age + purpose + current rabies record.

Step 1: confirm this is personal pet travel

CFIA separates personal pets from commercial imports such as certain dogs imported for resale, adoption, breeding or other commercial purposes. Commercial dog imports can have materially different paperwork and disease-risk rules.

This page is for an owner traveling with a personal dog or cat, not for a commercial import.

Step 2: check the pet's age

CFIA's current import reference states that domestic cats under 3 months of age are not subject to the standard rabies import restrictions. It also states that a dog under 3 months of age does not require rabies vaccination or certification that it came from a rabies-free country.

Once a dog or cat is 3 months of age or older, rabies documentation becomes the central requirement for the standard U.S.-origin personal-pet path.

Step 3: carry a current rabies record

For a dog 8 months of age or older arriving from a country Canada does not designate rabies-free, CFIA allows entry with a valid rabies vaccination certificate issued in English or French by a licensed veterinarian from the country of origin, clearly identifying the dog and showing that it is currently vaccinated against rabies. CFIA also recognizes an RNATT pathway in specified circumstances.

For domestic cats 3 months or older, CFIA likewise recognizes a current rabies vaccination certificate, a qualifying rabies-free origin, or an acceptable rabies neutralizing antibody titer result.

Practical record check: make sure the rabies certificate clearly identifies the same animal that is traveling. Keep the pet's name/description and any permanent identification information consistent across the record set.

Step 4: remember that transit through Canada still counts

CFIA says Canadian import requirements apply not only to animals entering permanently or temporarily, but also to animals in transit through Canada on the way to a final destination and to Canadian animals returning home.

If Canada is only a connection or overland transit point, do not assume the Canadian requirements disappear.

Step 5: do not confuse country-entry rules with airline rules

CFIA sets Canada's animal import requirements. An airline can add its own carrier, crate, reservation, breed or documentation rules, but an airline policy is not the legal authority for Canada's border-entry requirements.

For land crossings, the same principle applies: satisfy the Canadian import rules even when no airline is involved.

What if the pet does not meet the requirement?

CFIA says a dog that does not meet Canada's import requirements is reviewed at the border, and depending on the situation the animal may be ordered removed from Canada or other enforcement action may follow. The import reference also describes circumstances in which an inspector may order rabies vaccination at the owner's expense.

That is why checking the certificate before departure is safer than assuming a veterinarian or border officer can fix the situation on arrival.

U.S. → Canada checklist

  1. Confirm the trip is personal, not commercial.
  2. Confirm the pet's age. Under 3 months follows a different rabies path.
  3. Locate the current rabies certificate. For the standard vaccinated-pet pathway, make sure it is valid, readable and identifies the animal.
  4. Keep identification consistent. Carry microchip or other identifying information with the rabies record when available.
  5. Check the whole route. Canadian import rules can apply even when Canada is only a transit country.
  6. Check the carrier separately. Airline or rail/bus rules are additional to the border rules.

FAQ

Does my dog need a health certificate to enter Canada from the United States?

For a standard personal pet dog, Canada's animal-health import rules often center on rabies documentation rather than a general international health certificate. Your exact situation can differ based on age, purpose and travel arrangement, and a carrier can separately require its own health documentation.

Does my cat need a rabies certificate?

A domestic cat 3 months of age or older generally needs an acceptable rabies pathway under CFIA's import rules unless another listed option applies, such as qualifying rabies-free origin or an accepted RNATT result.

Are U.S. pets quarantined after entering Canada?

CFIA's current import reference specifically says pet dogs imported from any country are not subject to post-import quarantine. That does not waive the documentation requirements at entry.

Do Canada's rules apply if I am only driving through Canada?

Yes. CFIA says import requirements apply to animals in transit through Canada on the way to a final destination.
