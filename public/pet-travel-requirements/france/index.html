France Pet Travel Requirements From the United States

For non-commercial travel with a dog or cat from the United States to France, the core sequence is: identify the pet with a compliant microchip, keep rabies vaccination valid, use the correct EU health certificate, obtain USDA endorsement, and carry the original/required travel documents for arrival.

Reviewed by YourPetPass Travel Desk · Last reviewed September 28, 2026

Requirements at a glance

  • Species: this page covers non-commercial dogs and cats traveling from the United States to France.
  • Microchip: the pet must be identified with a compliant microchip before the rabies vaccination used for travel.
  • Rabies: the qualifying rabies vaccination must be valid. After a primary vaccination, the EU waiting period is at least 21 days.
  • Health certificate: U.S.-origin pets generally use the EU animal health certificate completed by a USDA-accredited veterinarian and endorsed by USDA unless a valid EU pet-passport exception applies.
  • October 2026 transition: USDA says the new non-commercial EU certificates become effective October 1, 2026; old certificates already in process can be endorsed on or before September 30.
  • France-specific issue: certain Category 1 dogs are prohibited from importation or introduction into France, including transit.

Direct answer: a dog or cat traveling from the United States to France for a non-commercial trip normally needs compliant identification, valid rabies vaccination, and an EU animal health certificate completed by a USDA-accredited veterinarian and endorsed by USDA before departure. The exact document and timing path changes if the pet qualifies to return on a valid EU pet passport or if the movement is commercial rather than non-commercial.

France follows the EU animal-health framework, but France also has national rules—especially for certain regulated dogs—that can change whether the trip is possible at all.

Step 1: confirm the trip is non-commercial

EU non-commercial pet movement is designed for pets traveling with their owner, or with an authorized person under the permitted timing relationship, and not for sale or transfer of ownership. The standard non-commercial framework generally covers no more than five dogs, cats or ferrets, subject to limited exceptions for qualifying events.

If the animal is traveling unaccompanied outside the permitted owner-travel relationship, is being sold or transferred, or otherwise falls outside the non-commercial rules, do not use this checklist as a substitute for the commercial-movement requirements.

Step 2: microchip before the rabies vaccination used for travel

EU rules require the pet's identification to be in place before, or where allowed at the same time as, the rabies vaccination that establishes travel eligibility. For most U.S. travelers, that means checking that the microchip number on the vaccination record and travel certificate matches the pet exactly.

Common failure point: a valid rabies shot can still create a travel problem if it predates the microchip used for the EU travel pathway or if the microchip number is recorded incorrectly.

Step 3: keep rabies vaccination valid—and count the 21 days correctly

For a primary rabies vaccination, EU validity for travel begins no sooner than 21 days after completion of the primary vaccination protocol. A subsequent vaccination can avoid restarting that waiting period when it is administered within the validity period of the preceding vaccination.

Keep the prior rabies certificate when a booster is being used to prove continuous coverage. The newest certificate alone may not show that the earlier vaccination was still valid on the booster date.

Step 4: use the correct 2026 EU health certificate

USDA APHIS updated its France guidance on September 1, 2026 because the EU certificate forms are changing. USDA states that new non-commercial health certificates are effective October 1, 2026. Old certificates already in process can still be endorsed on or before September 30, 2026.

The certificate should be completed by a USDA-accredited veterinarian and then endorsed by USDA APHIS when required for the U.S.-to-EU route. Do not use a generic certificate merely because it says “international health certificate”; the destination-specific EU certificate controls.

Step 5: know whether your pet can use an EU pet passport instead

An EU pet passport can replace the non-EU health certificate only in qualifying circumstances. The European Commission says the passport is intended for pets whose owners habitually reside in an EU country. For a pet temporarily outside the EU, a properly completed passport can support return when the required rabies conditions remain valid.

If the owner now habitually resides in the United States, do not assume an old EU pet passport remains the correct document just because it is still physically valid. Follow the current non-EU-entry rules unless the passport-use conditions are actually met.

Step 6: France has its own rules for certain dogs

French law imposes additional restrictions on dogs categorized as dangerous. France's agriculture ministry states that Category 1 dogs are prohibited from importation or introduction into France, including transit. Category 2 dogs may be introduced subject to specific requirements.

A dog without pedigree papers that resembles a Category 1 type can also create a classification problem. French guidance says an owner may need a veterinarian's signed attestation, translated into French if necessary, stating that the dog does not fall within the regulated categories. Classification is a France-specific legal issue separate from the EU health-certificate process.

What happens when you arrive in France?

France's current guidance says travelers arriving from third countries should present themselves to customs with the required original pet documents. France does not require pet owners to send those documents in advance or book a customs appointment merely to arrive with a qualifying non-commercial pet.

The EU also uses designated travelers' points of entry for many pets arriving from non-EU countries, with exceptions based on origin and other conditions. Confirm the current arrival point for the itinerary rather than assuming every airport handles pet entry identically.

U.S. → France checklist

  1. Confirm non-commercial status and who is traveling with the pet.
  2. Verify the microchip and make sure the number matches every travel record.
  3. Verify rabies timing and preserve prior certificates if a booster proves continuity.
  4. Use the correct EU certificate version for the travel date, including the October 2026 transition.
  5. Schedule a USDA-accredited veterinarian with enough time for USDA endorsement.
  6. Check France-specific dog restrictions before booking if the dog could fall into a regulated category.
  7. Carry the required originals/printed documents and verify the arrival point and customs process.

FAQ

Does my dog or cat need a rabies titer to enter France from the United States?

For a standard U.S.-origin pet traveling directly to France under the current EU listed-country framework, a rabies antibody titration test is generally not part of the normal path. The requirement can change when the pet's recent origin/history involves a country for which the EU requires titration, so route history matters.

Can I use an APHIS Form 7001 for France?

Do not treat Form 7001 as a universal substitute for the EU certificate. France/EU entry uses the applicable EU animal health certificate for this pathway.

How long after the first rabies vaccine can my pet travel?

Under the EU rules, at least 21 days must pass after completion of the primary rabies vaccination protocol before the vaccination becomes valid for travel, unless a different rule applies to the specific case.

Can my U.S. veterinarian issue the France certificate?

The U.S. veterinarian must be USDA-accredited for the export-health-certificate process, and the certificate requires USDA endorsement when the route requires it.

Are all dog breeds allowed into France?

No. France prohibits importation or introduction of Category 1 dogs, including transit. Category 2 dogs are permitted only subject to specific rules. If your dog could resemble a regulated type, review the French requirements before booking.
