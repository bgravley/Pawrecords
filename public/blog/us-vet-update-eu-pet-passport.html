Can a U.S. Vet Update an EU Pet Passport? What They Can—and Cannot—Enter

A U.S. veterinarian may be allowed to enter information in some EU pet passport sections, but not in sections reserved for an EU “Authorized Veterinarian.” Using the wrong section can invalidate the passport for travel.

By YourPetPass Travel Desk · September 28, 2026 · Sources reviewed September 28, 2026

The short answer

  • You cannot obtain an EU pet passport in the United States. EU pet passports are issued by authorized veterinarians in EU Member States or certain designated countries.
  • A licensed U.S. veterinarian can qualify as a “Veterinarian” for passport sections that permit that type of veterinarian.
  • A U.S. veterinarian must not write in a section reserved for an “Authorized Veterinarian.” USDA warns that doing so can invalidate the passport and lead to denied EU entry.
  • If an EU-recorded rabies vaccination is still valid during a qualifying temporary U.S. stay, the passport may remain sufficient for the return.
  • If that rabies vaccination expires and the pet is revaccinated in the United States, plan on an EU health certificate for the return trip.

Direct answer: a U.S. veterinarian can enter information only in EU pet passport sections that are open to a general “Veterinarian.” USDA APHIS distinguishes that term from “Authorized Veterinarian,” which specifically means an EU veterinarian. A U.S. veterinarian should never complete a section reserved for an Authorized Veterinarian.

Do not ask only, “Can my U.S. vet sign the passport?” Ask, “Which exact passport section is being completed, and who is authorized to complete that section?”

The two veterinarian labels are not interchangeable

USDA's current EU pet passport guidance says the passport itself identifies which type of veterinarian may complete each section. In that system, an Authorized Veterinarian is an EU veterinarian authorized under the EU rules, while a Veterinarian can include any licensed veterinarian, including a U.S. veterinarian.

That distinction is not clerical. USDA specifically warns U.S. veterinarians not to enter information in any passport section designated for an Authorized Veterinarian because doing so may invalidate the document for EU entry.

The rabies expiration date is the biggest decision point

For a pet temporarily in the United States, USDA says an EU pet passport can be used for the return to the EU when the qualifying rabies vaccination recorded in the passport by an EU veterinarian is still valid and the movement otherwise meets the non-commercial rules.

But if that rabies vaccination expires and the pet is revaccinated in the United States, USDA says the return path changes: the pet needs an EU health certificate completed by a USDA-accredited veterinarian and endorsed by USDA before departure.

What changes your answer? The owner's place of habitual residence, whether the pet is on a temporary trip or has effectively moved outside the EU, whether the EU-recorded rabies vaccination is still valid, which passport section needs an entry, and whether the movement is non-commercial can all change the correct document path.

An EU passport is not simply a document you keep forever and use from anywhere

The European Commission's current FAQ makes an important residence distinction: EU pet passports are intended for pets whose owners habitually reside in an EU country. If an owner now lives in a non-EU country, the Commission says the pet should follow the rules for entering the EU from that non-EU country rather than assuming an old EU passport remains the correct entry document.

That means nationality and property ownership are not enough by themselves. The Commission explicitly says habitual residence is what matters for this passport-use question.

A practical U.S.-vet workflow

  1. Open the passport before the appointment. Identify the exact section that needs a new entry.
  2. Read the veterinarian label for that section. Determine whether it says Authorized Veterinarian or Veterinarian.
  3. Check the rabies validity date. If the EU-recorded vaccination is still valid, do not replace it casually with a U.S. entry without first understanding the travel consequence.
  4. If rabies coverage expired, switch workflows. Ask a USDA-accredited veterinarian about the current EU health-certificate process rather than trying to make the passport carry a U.S. revaccination it cannot validly document for EU re-entry.
  5. Confirm your residence status. If you now live outside the EU, check the non-EU-entry rules even if you still possess an EU passport.
  6. Carry the supporting records. Keep the passport, rabies certificates, microchip documentation and any EU health certificate together for travel.

FAQ

Can I get an EU pet passport from a veterinarian in the United States?

No. USDA says EU pet passports are not issued in the United States. They are issued by an official or authorized veterinarian in an EU Member State or another qualifying jurisdiction under the EU system.

Can my U.S. vet write a rabies booster into my EU pet passport?

Do not assume so. The passport specifies which type of veterinarian may complete each section. USDA warns that U.S. veterinarians must not enter information in sections reserved for an EU Authorized Veterinarian. If the EU-recorded rabies vaccination has expired and the pet is revaccinated in the United States, USDA says an EU health certificate is required for the return.

What if my pet's EU rabies vaccination is still valid?

For a qualifying non-commercial return after a temporary U.S. stay, USDA says the valid EU-recorded rabies vaccination can allow the pet to travel back using the EU pet passport, provided the other applicable conditions are met.

I moved permanently to the United States but still have an EU pet passport. Can I use it?

The European Commission says the passport is intended for pets whose owners habitually reside in an EU country. If you now live in a non-EU country, follow the EU rules for entry from that non-EU country rather than relying on the passport alone.
