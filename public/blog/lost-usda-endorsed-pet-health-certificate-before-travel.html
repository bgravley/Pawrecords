Lost Your USDA-Endorsed Pet Health Certificate Before Travel? What to Do

A missing certificate does not have one universal fix. The right recovery path depends on whether the destination accepts a digitally endorsed certificate or requires an original hard copy—and whether the document was lost before or after USDA endorsement.

By YourPetPass Travel Desk · September 22, 2026 · Sources reviewed September 22, 2026

The short answer

  • If a mailed pet health-certificate package is missing, USDA APHIS currently tells owners to contact the courier; lost packages must be reissued through VEHCS by the veterinarian.
  • Do not assume a phone photo, PDF or ordinary photocopy can replace the required travel document. USDA says the original signed or endorsed paperwork must accompany the pet when the destination requires it.
  • Some destinations accept USDA digital endorsement, which can change the recovery path because the endorsed certificate can be printed from VEHCS. Other destinations still require a USDA-endorsed hard copy with an ink signature and embossed seal.
  • Your first call should usually be to the USDA-accredited veterinarian who submitted the certificate. USDA says veterinarians submit documents and can track endorsement status in VEHCS.

Direct answer: if your U.S. pet's international health certificate disappears before departure, first determine whether it was never returned, lost in shipping, or lost after you received it. Then confirm whether the destination accepts a printed digitally endorsed certificate or requires the original USDA-endorsed paper document. USDA's current notice says a lost mailed package must be reissued through VEHCS by your veterinarian. citeturn2search1

The key question is not simply “Do I have a copy?” It is “Does my destination accept this version of the certificate?”

Why a saved PDF may not solve the problem

USDA's Pet Travel Process Overview says the original signed or endorsed paperwork must accompany the pet during travel and warns that electronic versions are not accepted by destination officials when original paperwork is required. For destinations that require a physical USDA endorsement, the original hard copy can therefore be the travel document—not merely evidence that the document once existed. citeturn2search3

But there is an important exception to the workflow: USDA says some countries accept certificates that are digitally endorsed in VEHCS. In those cases, the certificate can be issued, endorsed and returned electronically, and the traveler may be able to print the endorsed certificate rather than wait for an embossed original to be shipped. citeturn2search11

What changes your answer? Destination country, species, certificate type, whether USDA endorsement is required, whether that destination accepts digital endorsement, and how much validity time remains. Do not apply another country's document rule to your trip.

A practical recovery workflow

  1. Find the tracking number. If a physical package is missing, check the courier status first. USDA specifically directs owners to the courier for missing mailed documents.
  2. Call the USDA-accredited veterinarian. USDA says the veterinarian submits documents and can track the endorsement in VEHCS. Pet owners should not try to create a second submission themselves.
  3. Open the current USDA destination page. Confirm whether the country requires USDA endorsement and whether it accepts digital endorsement or needs a hard-copy endorsement.
  4. Calculate the remaining validity window. A reissued document still has to satisfy the destination's examination, certificate, treatment, testing and arrival deadlines. Reissuing paperwork does not automatically reset every underlying requirement.
  5. Ask whether supporting originals must also travel. Some destinations require additional original vaccination or laboratory records alongside the endorsed certificate.
  6. Build a backup plan before the deadline becomes impossible. USDA currently advises travelers to plan for the possibility that a certificate may not be ready, including changing travel dates or arranging a caretaker when necessary.

Do not create a duplicate submission on your own

USDA's current pet-travel notice says pet owners cannot submit documents directly to USDA offices; submissions must be made by the veterinarian through VEHCS. USDA's process overview likewise describes the accredited veterinarian as the party who submits the certificate through VEHCS on the owner's behalf. citeturn2search1turn2search3

That matters because a missing package can tempt a traveler to start over independently. The cleaner recovery path is to have the submitting veterinarian determine the certificate's actual status and coordinate any reissue.

Example: why destination rules change the answer

Japan illustrates the difference. USDA says dogs and cats can use digitally endorsed certificates that the accredited veterinarian prints after APHIS endorsement, while certain other pet types—including pet birds and ferrets—require the APHIS veterinary officer's ink signature and embossed seal on the paper certificate. The same destination can therefore have different document-handling rules depending on species. citeturn2search18

China provides another reason not to travel with only a certificate PDF: USDA says dogs and cats need a printed APHIS-endorsed health certificate and must also arrive with an original current rabies vaccination certificate and the rabies-titer laboratory report. citeturn2search13

Before you leave for the airport

  • Confirm the endorsed certificate version in your hand is the version the destination accepts.
  • Confirm every required supporting original or printed document is present.
  • Keep the physical travel packet in your carry-on or other owner-controlled document folder—not checked baggage.
  • Keep digital copies separately for reference and recovery, while recognizing they may not substitute for required originals.
  • Recheck the destination's official requirements shortly before departure because USDA notes that country requirements can change.

FAQ

Can I travel with a photocopy of a lost USDA-endorsed certificate?

Do not assume so. USDA says original signed or endorsed paperwork must accompany the pet when the destination requires it. If the destination accepts digital endorsement, the proper printed digitally endorsed certificate may be acceptable; confirm the destination-specific USDA instructions.

Who should I call if FedEx, UPS or another courier loses the certificate?

USDA says to contact the courier for missing mailed documents and states that lost packages must be reissued through VEHCS by the veterinarian. Contact the submitting USDA-accredited veterinarian at the same time.

Can USDA email me another copy?

The answer depends on the certificate and destination. Some destinations accept digital USDA endorsement and allow the endorsed certificate to be printed from VEHCS. Others require a hard-copy endorsement with ink signature and embossed seal. Your accredited veterinarian should confirm the applicable route.

Does reissuing the certificate restart the travel clock?

Not necessarily. Destination rules can tie validity to examination dates, treatments, testing, certificate issuance, endorsement or arrival. Reissuance should be checked against every applicable timing requirement rather than treated as an automatic reset.
