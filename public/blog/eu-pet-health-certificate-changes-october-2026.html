EU Pet Health Certificate Changes: October 2026 Rules Now in Effect

The October 1 non-commercial and October 17 commercial certificate changes are now in effect. Here is what to check before your pet travels from the U.S. to the EU.

By YourPetPass Travel Desk · Updated October 18, 2026

The short answer

Both transition dates have passed. For new U.S.-to-EU paperwork, ask your USDA-accredited veterinarian to use the current certificate for your destination and movement type. An old PDF saved from a previous trip is not a reliable starting point.

What changed on October 1 and October 17?

USDA APHIS gives two U.S. endorsement cutoffs:

MovementNew form effectiveOld form endorsement cutoff
Non-commercial pet travelOctober 1, 2026September 30, 2026
Commercial dogs, cats and ferretsOctober 17, 2026October 16, 2026

Those are form-transition dates, not a fresh travel-validity period. The European Commission says old non-commercial certificates issued before October 1 and old commercial certificates issued before October 17 remain eligible under the transition provisions. They still need to satisfy the applicable validity and entry conditions. A pre-cutoff signature does not make a certificate valid indefinitely.

Which certificate applies to your pet?

Non-commercial travel generally means your pet is not being sold or transferred to someone else, and its movement is connected to your own journey. The EU permits an authorised person to accompany your pet when you travel within five days before or after it, with the required declaration. More than five pets normally triggers a different pathway, with a limited exception for qualifying events.

Unaccompanied transport, rehoming and journeys outside those conditions need individual review. Give your veterinarian the owner’s itinerary, the pet’s itinerary and any transporter details before choosing a form. A cargo booking alone is not enough information to classify the trip.

Certificate timing still matters

For the U.S. non-commercial process described by APHIS, the accredited veterinarian’s certificate has a 30-day validity period and USDA endorsement must occur within 10 days of EU arrival. These are separate steps. The EU describes a 10-day period to entry checks following official issue; onward EU travel can be covered for up to six months after entry checks, limited by rabies-vaccination expiry. Confirm the exact schedule with your accredited veterinarian.

Commercial movements use a different process, including an examination close to dispatch, border-control arrangements and import documentation. The Commission’s commercial entry guidance explains the Border Control Post and Common Health Entry Document requirements. Do not apply an ordinary holiday checklist to a commercial movement.

What to do before the appointment

  1. Write down the complete route. Include transit airports, arrival dates, onward destinations and who accompanies your pet.
  2. Ask the veterinarian to select the current form. Use the destination’s current APHIS guidance and VEHCS process, not an old download.
  3. Bring the supporting history. Include microchip identification, rabies records and any required test or treatment documentation.
  4. Confirm the endorsement plan and travel copy. APHIS specifies a printed endorsed certificate travelling with the pet; a phone copy is a useful backup, not a substitute.
  5. Check the final document. Compare names, microchip number, dates and itinerary with the original records. Ask the issuing veterinarian to handle corrections.

Does the new form replace the health requirements?

No. Identification, valid rabies vaccination and any route-specific testing or treatment still matter. A form change alone is not a reason to repeat a vaccination. Have your veterinarian check your pet’s records and timing. See our microchip guide and USDA endorsement guide for the surrounding steps.

What about an EU airport connection?

Transit and entry are different. For qualifying non-commercial travel, remaining in the international transit zone can avoid EU entry requirements. Confirm where your pet will actually stay: an airport pet hotel may be outside that zone. Read the EU pet layover guide and verify the arrangement with the port officials.
