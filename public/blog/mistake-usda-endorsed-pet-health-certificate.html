Mistake on a USDA-Endorsed Pet Health Certificate? What to Do Next

The right fix depends on when you catch the error. Once USDA has endorsed an international health certificate, editing the document yourself is not the solution.

By YourPetPass Travel Desk · September 21, 2026 · Sources reviewed September 21, 2026

The short answer

  • If the certificate has been submitted but not endorsed: have your USDA-accredited veterinarian contact the USDA Endorsement Office immediately. USDA says a submission may be returned in VEHCS for correction before review is completed.
  • If USDA has already endorsed it: do not alter the endorsed certificate. USDA's VEHCS training says information that must change after endorsement requires the certificate to be reissued; additional endorsement fees may apply.
  • Do not assume every typo is harmless. Animal identification, microchip, vaccination, testing, treatment and owner/export details can be material because the certificate must match the supporting records and destination requirements.
  • Recheck the travel clock. Reissuing paperwork does not necessarily reset or extend the destination country's examination, treatment, certificate or arrival windows.

Direct answer: if you discover an error on a U.S. international pet health certificate, first determine whether USDA APHIS has endorsed it. Before endorsement, the veterinarian may be able to correct a returned VEHCS submission. After endorsement, USDA instructs users not to make corrections to the endorsed certificate; when information must change, the certificate may need to be reissued through VEHCS. Contact the accredited veterinarian and USDA Endorsement Office rather than editing the official document.

The safest question is not “Can I fix this typo?” It is “What stage is the certificate in, and does the corrected document still meet the destination's clock?”

Errors that deserve immediate attention

USDA tells accredited veterinarians to make sure the animal's description, identification, vaccination, testing and treatments match the medical records and supporting documentation exactly. That makes discrepancies in these fields especially important to escalate.

Check these first: microchip number; species/breed/sex/color when required; rabies vaccine date, product and validity; laboratory result/date; parasite-treatment date/product; owner or exporter information; destination; certificate dates; and any permit number that must match the travel package.

A formatting rule can also matter when the destination specifies one. Mexico, for example, currently tells U.S. travelers that its health certificate must be typewritten/computer-completed and will not accept handmade corrections, erasures, lineouts or crossouts. That is a destination-specific requirement, not a universal correction rule.

A six-step correction workflow

  1. Stop editing. Do not write over, white-out, cross out or otherwise alter an endorsed certificate yourself.
  2. Identify the exact mismatch. Compare the certificate field with the underlying veterinary record, vaccine certificate, microchip documentation, laboratory report or import permit.
  3. Confirm certificate status. Is it still with the veterinarian, submitted in VEHCS, returned for correction, or already endorsed?
  4. Contact the USDA-accredited veterinarian. For VEHCS submissions, the veterinarian is normally the person who can correct/resubmit or initiate the reissue process.
  5. Escalate to the USDA Endorsement Office when needed. USDA specifically recommends contacting the office about the reason for reissuing an endorsed certificate, particularly if the animal is already being held in another country.
  6. Recalculate every deadline. Verify that the corrected/reissued certificate, examination, treatments, tests, permits and new arrival date still satisfy the destination's current rules.

Why timing becomes the second problem

A document correction can consume time inside an already narrow travel window. USDA APHIS notes that incomplete or incorrect health certificates delay endorsement, and that once an accredited veterinarian signs a certificate, the destination may allow only a limited period for endorsement and travel.

So a successful correction does not automatically mean the trip is ready. After the certificate is returned or reissued, check the destination's current government requirements again. If a timed examination, parasite treatment or certificate-validity window has moved outside the permitted range, another step may need to be repeated.

What not to do

  • Do not alter a USDA-endorsed certificate yourself.
  • Do not create a replacement PDF or scan that makes an altered document look original.
  • Do not assume a small identification mismatch is automatically acceptable at the border.
  • Do not ask an airline to decide whether a country's legal entry certificate is valid; use the destination authority and USDA process for country-entry documentation.
  • Do not discard the superseded certificate or supporting records unless your veterinarian/USDA process instructs you to; keep the document trail organized.

FAQ

Can I correct a USDA-endorsed pet health certificate by hand?

No. USDA VEHCS training says corrections cannot be made to an endorsed health certificate. If information needs to change after endorsement, the certificate may be reissued in VEHCS. Contact your accredited veterinarian and USDA Endorsement Office.

What if I catch the mistake before USDA endorses it?

Act immediately. USDA says that if an error is noticed after VEHCS submission, the Endorsement Office may be able to return it for correction before review is completed. USDA may also return a submission when its review finds an error.

Will USDA fix the electronic certificate for my veterinarian?

USDA's VEHCS training states APHIS is unable to make changes to electronically submitted documents. The submission is returned so the appropriate correction can be made and resubmitted.

Will I have to pay another endorsement fee?

Possibly. USDA's VEHCS training says endorsement fees may apply when an endorsed certificate is reissued.

Does reissuing the certificate give me a new travel window?

Do not assume that it does. The destination country defines the applicable timing rules, and other requirements—such as examinations, treatments or tests—may have clocks independent of certificate reissuance.
