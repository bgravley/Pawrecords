Pet Health Records: What to Keep and How to Organize Them

Your pet’s history can be scattered across clinics, emails and paper folders. Build one clear collection for your next vet visit, move or trip.

By YourPetPass Health Records Team · Updated October 21, 2026

Start with the records you already have

Keep copies of veterinary visit notes, vaccination certificates, test results, medication instructions and your pet’s identification. Add new documents after each visit and keep the originals unchanged. A short summary helps you find key facts; it should sit alongside the full records.

What belongs in your pet’s health history?

RecordWhat to save
Identity and contactsPet’s name, species, date of birth if known, microchip number, your contact details and clinic contacts.
Vaccination evidenceCertificates and clinic records showing the vaccine, date and relevant identifying details. A reminder email is not the same as a certificate.
Visits and proceduresExamination notes, diagnoses, surgery and dental records, and discharge instructions.
Tests and imagingLaboratory reports, imaging reports and access to the original images when the receiving clinic needs them.
Medications and reactionsCurrent written instructions, prescription labels and recorded allergies or past reactions. Keep discontinued treatments clearly separate.
Travel documentsHealth certificates, permits and supporting test or treatment records, grouped by trip.

This is an organization checklist, not a list of tests or treatments your pet needs. Your veterinarian decides what care is appropriate.

Ask each clinic for the missing pieces

A regular veterinarian, emergency hospital and specialist may each hold different records. Ask for the complete clinical record and relevant attachments, then check what arrived. A payment receipt may list services without the clinical findings.

The AVMA’s guide to choosing a veterinarian recommends transferring copies to the new practice when you move. Ask the clinic about its release process, timing and any fees; requirements vary by location. Use our Medical Records Transfer Checklist for the request and follow-up steps.

Make the collection easy to use

  1. Give each pet a separate home for records. Keep documents for pets with similar names clearly separated.
  2. Name files consistently. For example: 2026-10-18_Sadie_ClinicName_LabReport.pdf. Preserve the clinic’s original content.
  3. Group by purpose. Use categories such as vaccines, visits, tests, medications and travel.
  4. Keep a current overview. Note the date you last checked it and mark anything you still need to confirm.
  5. Back up essential copies. Test that you can open them when you need them, including away from your usual device.

After a visit, save the new report, check whether instructions changed, and set aside any questions for the clinic. Do not silently correct a medical entry yourself. Ask the issuing clinic to clarify or amend it.

What to do when two records disagree

Keep both original documents and ask the issuing clinic to clarify the difference. A newer download date does not establish which medical instruction is current. Until the clinic confirms it, label the disputed detail as unconfirmed in your own summary instead of silently selecting one version.

What you findYour next step
A summary and a prescription label show different directionsSend both to the prescribing clinic and ask which instructions apply now. Do not change a dose based on your own comparison. Keep the clinic's response with the records.
A scanned name, date or number looks different from the extracted textCompare the text against the original page. If the scan is unclear, request a readable copy. Mark the uncertain field; do not fill it from memory.
Two files have the same title but different contentsKeep the clinic, visit date and received date in your file index. Ask whether one is an amendment or whether each contains different information. Preserve both originals.
A report mentions an attachment you cannot findRecord the missing attachment's name and visit date, then ask the issuing clinic for it. Use the transfer checklist to track the request and confirm receipt.

For medication questions, FDA's medication-error guidance emphasizes clear directions and discussing medicines with your veterinarian. Use the medication list guide to record the confirmed plan; the filing workflow here does not determine treatment.

Keep a short clarification log

For each unresolved detail, note: pet; documents compared; exact question; clinic contacted; request date; reply; and date your summary was updated. For example: “Two reports show different visit dates; asked the clinic to confirm.” Keep the answer with the source documents and update your overview only when the question is resolved.

Share what the next person needs

A new veterinarian may need the full history. A boarding provider may only ask for vaccination evidence. Confirm the recipient and send the relevant documents through the method they request. Avoid posting identifiable medical records on a public page.

For urgent care, keep your regular clinic’s contact details and a short medical summary accessible. Do not delay urgent veterinary care while collecting files. For a move, read switching vets without losing the history.

Are digital copies enough for travel?

They help you organize and recover information, but they do not replace official documents where an authority requires them. Check the destination and carrier rules.
