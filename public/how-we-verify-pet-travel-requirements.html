Editorial methodology

How YourPetPass Verifies Pet Travel Requirements

Pet travel rules can change by country, species, origin, route, date and travel arrangement. Our goal is to make those rules easier to use without hiding the conditions that change the answer.

YourPetPass treats pet travel requirements as a source-and-scope problem, not a generic checklist problem. We research the responsible authority, preserve the conditions attached to each rule, record when the source was reviewed, and separate legal entry requirements from airline or airport policies.

Our source hierarchy

Country entry, export, transit, quarantine, health, vaccination, treatment, permit and customs rules: responsible national or regional government authority first.

Airline rules: the airline's own policy for carriage, service animals, pet reservations, crates and operational requirements.

Airport logistics: the airport or official airport operator for relief areas, terminal access and connection procedures.

Supplemental industry sources: used only when they add operational context and do not replace the legal authority.

What we record for a travel rule

Jurisdiction

The country, region, territory or shared regulatory system that owns the rule.

Species

Dog, cat, ferret or another animal category when the rule differs by species.

Origin

Where the animal is coming from or has recently been, when that changes the pathway.

Requirement type

Microchip, rabies, titer, certificate, treatment, permit, quarantine, arrival check or another defined requirement.

Timing

The event the clock is measured from—vaccination, certificate issue, treatment, departure or arrival.

Source + review date

The official source used and the date the rule was last checked.

Why a country page is not automatically a universal answer

A destination page is useful only when its scope is clear. The same country can have different rules for:

Different origins: rabies-risk classification or disease restrictions can change the required documents.
Different species: dogs and cats may share a pathway while birds or ferrets use another.
Different movement types: accompanied, authorized-person, unaccompanied and commercial movements may be treated differently.
Different dates: new certificate forms, transitional periods or temporary disease controls can change the answer.
Different routes: transit through another jurisdiction can create an additional requirement.

How we handle EU rules

We do not independently rewrite the same shared EU rule for every Member State and pretend it is a separate national rule. Shared EU requirements are treated as a common baseline. Country pages add the national differences that matter—for example, local dog restrictions, entry-point procedures or other country-specific conditions.

This reduces duplication and makes it easier to revalidate shared rules when the EU framework changes.

Freshness and rule changes

Official websites can leave older pages or PDFs online after newer guidance is published. We therefore do not assume every official page has equal current authority. When current guidance conflicts with an older source, the newer governing material should be identified and the older material treated as superseded rather than blended into one answer.

Our public travel-rule change ledger is one way we track material changes that can affect existing guidance.

What AI does—and does not do

YourPetPass may use AI to help research, organize and summarize travel requirements, but AI output is not itself the legal authority. A rule becomes suitable for a public destination page only after the relevant official sources are identified, the scope is clear, duplicate intent is checked, and the information is reviewed for publication.

We do not intentionally create an indexable page for every possible route permutation. A separate public page should add a real regulatory distinction or useful decision, not just a different keyword combination.

What owners should still verify

Travel requirements can change after a page is reviewed. Before travel, confirm the current requirements with the responsible government authority for the destination and any relevant origin/transit jurisdiction. Separately confirm the carrier's own policy if traveling by air, rail, ferry or another commercial carrier.

Editorial identity

Travel content may be published under organizational editorial identities such as YourPetPass Travel Desk. These are editorial teams, not individual veterinarians or government officials. YourPetPass does not claim veterinary licensure or government authority.
