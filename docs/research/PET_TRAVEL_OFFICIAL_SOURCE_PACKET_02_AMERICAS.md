## Canada

Primary authority: Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA).

Research notes:
- Canada requirements are situation-specific and cover temporary visits, permanent imports, transit and returning Canadian animals.
- CFIA defines dogs, cats and ferrets as pets for this workflow; other species use other import pathways.
- Dog rules vary by age, purpose and rabies status/origin. Commercial dogs from countries CFIA identifies as high-risk for dog rabies are subject to a separate prohibition.
- Pet dogs imported from any country are not subject to post-import quarantine under the cited CFIA reference document.
- Do not flatten commercial and personal pet-dog rules into one guide.

Official sources:

- https://inspection.canada.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/imports

## Mexico

Primary authority: SENASICA.

Research notes:
- SENASICA published refreshed dog/cat travel guidance on 2026-08-04 and a refreshed Pets landing page on 2026-09-11; older pet-travel pages conflict with newer guidance and must not be treated as current merely because they remain online.
- Current SENASICA guidance says arriving dogs/cats request a Zoosanitary Import Certificate (CZI) at the point of entry and undergo physical inspection.
- Current general visitor guidance states a good-health certificate and vaccination record are not necessary, but documentary requirements vary by origin.
- For USA-origin travel, current SENASICA guidance includes New World screwworm documentation for dogs (cats exempt in the cited USA section), signed by a USDA-accredited veterinarian within 5 days before travel.
- This is a strong freshness/country-origin example: the knowledge system must prefer current official guidance and preserve origin-specific overrides.

## Costa Rica

Primary authority: SENASA Costa Rica.

Research notes:
- Dogs/cats must have a clinical examination by a veterinarian in the origin country within the two weeks before travel.
- SENASA states they must carry an official health certificate issued by the competent health authority of the origin/provenance country.
- Dogs/cats older than 3 months must have rabies vaccination.
- Species-specific core vaccinations are listed by SENASA.
- Internal and external parasite treatment is required within 15 days before arrival, with treatment details documented.
- Pets imported as cargo require a prior import permit through a customs agency in addition to the health requirements.
- Transit up to 24 hours has a separate SENASA border-inspection/authorization workflow.

Official sources:
- https://www.senasa.go.cr/informacion/noticias/617-conozca-los-requisitos-sanitarios-establecidos-por-senasa-para-la-importacion-de-perros-y-gatos

## Colombia

Primary authority: Instituto Colombiano Agropecuario (ICA).

Research notes:
- ICA's current workflow covers dogs/cats entering as accompanied baggage or cargo.
- The owner/responsible person uses ICA/SISPAP to request the Certificado de Inspección Sanitaria (CIS).
- ICA requires an original health certificate issued or endorsed by the official sanitary authority of the origin country, issued no more than 10 calendar days before entry.
- The certificate includes animal identification/description, health status, fitness for transport and internal/external antiparasitic treatment within 60 days before travel.
- ICA performs documentary and physical inspection and issues the CIS when satisfactory.
- ICA explicitly states its pet entry/exit certification does not certify or accredit service, emotional-support or assistance-animal status. Keep legal health-entry rules separate from airline/service-animal policy.

Official sources:
- https://www.ica.gov.co/importacion-y-exportacion/otros-procedimientos/requisitos-para-importar-mascotas
- https://www.ica.gov.co/importacion-y-exportacion/otros-procedimientos/requisitos-para-importar-mascotas/ingreso-de-perros-y-gatos-a-colombia

## Ecuador

Primary authority: Agrocalidad.

Research notes:
- Dogs/cats entering Ecuador require a Zoosanitary Export Certificate issued by the official veterinary service of the origin country, in Spanish or English, meeting Ecuador's sanitary requirements.
- Arrival mode materially changes the workflow. A dog/cat arriving with the passenger in cabin / baggage-arrival flow does not need the separate Zoosanitary Import Permit described for cargo, but still requires the official export certificate.
- Cargo arrival requires a Zoosanitary Import Permit and advance document handling; Agrocalidad's current page specifies documents sent 72 hours before arrival.
- Agrocalidad's current import page says a pet temporarily leaving Ecuador may use the same Ecuador-issued export certificate to return when no more than 30 calendar days have elapsed from issuance.
- Current Ecuador source material shows version drift in older PDFs (including age differences), so normalization must use the current governing requirement/source and mark superseded source copies stale rather than blending them.
- Ecuador export guidance says the registered veterinarian submits the export-certificate request 72 hours before travel; the animal-health certification has a 10-day validity. Destination-country requirements remain controlling for destination entry.

Official sources:
- https://www.agrocalidad.gob.ec/ingreso-al-ecuador-de-animales-de-compania-perros-y-gatos/
- https://www.agrocalidad.gob.ec/salida-de-animales-de-compania-perros-y-gatos/
- https://www.agrocalidad.gob.ec/exportacion-e-importacion-de-mascotas/

## Cross-system findings

1. **Origin overrides are essential.** Mexico's current USA-origin screwworm requirement is a concrete example.
2. **Transport mode can change legal workflow.** Ecuador and Costa Rica both distinguish cargo in meaningful ways.
3. **Freshness needs source-version handling.** Mexico has old official pages that conflict with 2026 guidance; Ecuador has older official PDFs with differing age language.
4. **Service-animal status must not leak into health-entry logic.** Colombia explicitly separates ICA health certification from assistance/service-animal accreditation.
5. **Transit deserves structured scope.** Canada applies import rules to transit; Costa Rica documents a specific short-transit authorization workflow.
6. These findings support normalized rules + scoped overrides rather than static prose-only country pages.
